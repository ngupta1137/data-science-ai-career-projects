# Model card: claims frequency and severity pricing

Generated by `python -m claims_pricing.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for pricing auto insurance policies by segment using a two-part frequency-severity model under exposure duration weighting. Not an actuarial rate filing or production underwriting system.

## Data
30000 training policies (16501.8 policy-years exposure, 2404 claims, 3508654.71 total losses) and 20000 holdout policies (10967.4 policy-years exposure, 1621 claims, 2332862.49 total losses). See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
Target portfolio loss ratio 0.70 (claims / premium), maximum sustainable top-decile loss ratio 0.95, minimum acceptable Gini 0.25. All candidate models are evaluated on identical holdout policies.

## Candidate models
Technical charged premium is pure premium times exposure divided by target loss ratio.

| Model | Gini coefficient | Overall loss ratio | Pure premium RMSE | Top-decile loss ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat_baseline | 0.0000 | 0.7003 | 130.97 | 1.6061 |
| no_exposure | 0.3313 | 1.2706 | 109.15 | 1.2277 |
| single_tweedie | 0.3303 | 0.6959 | 18.99 | 0.6813 |
| freq_sev_two_part | 0.3315 | 0.6973 | 15.53 | 0.6777 |
| oracle | 0.3335 | 0.7146 | 0.00 | 0.6816 |

- `flat_baseline`: uniform portfolio average pure premium rate for all risks.
- `no_exposure`: fits claim count without exposure offset or weighting.
- `single_tweedie`: compound Poisson-Gamma Tweedie regression (p=1.5) on pure premium.
- `freq_sev_two_part`: Poisson GLM with log-exposure offset times Gamma severity GLM. Headline model, fixed before results.
- `oracle`: true simulator expected frequency times true severity. Upper bound.

## The traps, measured
- Ignoring exposure time: policies with short exposure (< 0.30 yr, n=4571) experience a loss ratio of 1.1300 under the unweighted count model versus 0.6202 under the two-part model. Full exposure policies (> 0.80 yr, n=4484) experience 1.2048 versus 0.6612. The model that ignores exposure time misprices short-duration policies because it treats raw claim count as the annualized rate.
- Loss ratio by decile: under flat pricing, decile 1 has loss ratio 0.1902 (severe overcharging) while decile 10 has loss ratio 1.6061 (underwriting loss). Under two-part pricing, decile 1 is 0.6237 and decile 10 is 0.6777. Flat pricing heavily overcharges the safest segment and undercharges the riskiest segment, resulting in an unsustainable top-decile loss ratio.
- One model for frequency and size: single Tweedie pure premium RMSE is 18.99 (Gini 0.3303) compared to two-part RMSE of 15.53 (Gini 0.3315). Paired Gini difference 95% interval: +0.000136 to +0.002167. Fitting a single compound Tweedie model conflates frequency drivers with severity drivers, reducing pricing discrimination and increasing pure premium error.

## Limits
- Everything is simulated. Driver risk parameters, vehicle values, and accident distributions are chosen by the author.
- Exposure is simulated uniformly from 0.1 to 1.0 year without seasonality or cancellation correlation.
- Individual claims are assumed independent and follow a Gamma distribution. Real severity often has heavy Pareto tails.
- Features are limited to five synthetic variables without interaction terms or telematics data.
- Intervals resample holdout policies. They do not cover model misspecification or a changing underlying environment.
