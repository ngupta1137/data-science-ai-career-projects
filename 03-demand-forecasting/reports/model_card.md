# Model card: demand forecasting

Generated by `python -m demand_forecast.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for evaluating a forecast with a rolling-origin backtest and honest features. Not for operating a real bike fleet.

## Data
Daily bike rental counts, 731 days. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Backtest design
Forecast 7 days ahead. 52 rolling origins starting after 365 days of history, one every 7 days. The first 26 origins tune hyperparameters and pick the headline model and best baseline. The last 26 origins (182 days) are the test, scored once.

## Results
Counts are rentals per day. MASE divides MAE by the in-sample MAE of a 7 day seasonal naive forecast (866.6); below 1 beats that benchmark.

Headline: **ridge**, test MAE 984.6, 95% interval 778.0 to 1236.5 (block bootstrap over 26 forecast blocks, 1000 resamples). Best baseline: baseline_mean_28, test MAE 961.0. Gap (baseline minus headline) 95% interval: -95.9 to 41.6.

## The traps, measured
- Same-day components: MAE 47.8 with the leak versus 984.6 honest. casual + registered equals the target on the same day, so the model reads the answer.
- Short lags: MAE 807.8 versus 984.6 honest. lag 1 and lag 2 are unknown 7 days ahead. Using them scores a forecast nobody could make.
- Shuffled folds: MAE 782.2 on random 5-fold versus 984.6 on the rolling-origin test. Shuffled folds train on days after the day being scored. Different rows, so this compares designs.

## Where it fails
Worst weekday by MAE: **6** (0 is Sunday). Median fold MAE 777.0, worst fold 3090.5. Full tables are in `results/error_analysis.json`.

## Limits
- The data is two years of one city. It cannot show how a model behaves over more years or in other places.
- Weather is excluded because it is not known 7 days ahead. A real system would use weather forecasts, which this project does not.
- Forecast intervals are not produced. Point forecasts only.
- Only about half a year of test blocks is scored, so intervals are wide.
- The same-day and short-lag variants are deliberately wrong. Their scores are shown to measure the damage, not as results.
