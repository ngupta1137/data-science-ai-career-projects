# Model card: fairness and model risk audit

Generated by `python -m fairness_audit.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for auditing a decision policy by group: measure, find the gap, try a mitigation, and see what it costs. Not a compliance tool and not legal advice.

## Data
Simulated applicants with known mechanisms (6000 fit, 3000 dev, 6000 test rows). Group B is 0.25 of the test set. Base rate of the good outcome: group A 0.511, group B 0.382. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
Approving a good applicant is worth 1; approving a bad one costs 2. The tolerance on the true positive rate gap is 0.1. Both are assumptions. Gaps are group B minus group A.

## The traps, measured
- Unawareness: TPR gap -0.254 with the group column, -0.156 without it, -0.149 without it and without the proxy feature. Dropping the group column, and even the proxy, leaves a gap. The gap comes from different base rates and a noisier measurement for group B.
- One AUC: overall 0.750, group A 0.755, group B 0.714. One overall number averages over a group the model serves worse.
- Thresholds tuned on test: TPR gap +0.002 versus -0.084 when tuned on dev. Thresholds fitted on the rows they are scored on look perfect by construction.
- A zero gap is cheap: rejecting everyone has TPR gap +0.000 and utility 0.000. A policy that approves nobody has no gap. A fairness number needs a utility number beside it.
- Not everything can be equal: with base rates 0.511 and 0.382, group thresholds move the gaps from TPR -0.156, FPR -0.052, PPV -0.109 to TPR -0.084, FPR -0.002, PPV -0.147. With different base rates, equalizing the true positive rate moves the other gaps. They cannot all be zero together.
- The small group: the 95% interval on group B's true positive rate is 0.073 wide against 0.041 for group A.

## Limits
- All data is simulated with mechanisms chosen by the author. The sizes of the gaps describe this setup, not any real lending or hiring system.
- Two groups only. No intersections, no more than one protected attribute, and no small-subgroup problem beyond group B being a quarter of the data.
- The group label is observed and correct. In practice it is often missing, inferred, or legally off limits.
- The 0.10 tolerance and the cost of a bad approval are assumptions. Change them and the verdict can change; the sensitivity table shows how.
- Equal opportunity is one definition among several. Satisfying it does not make a policy fair in a legal or ethical sense. This project gives no legal advice and no compliance determination.
- The outcome label is taken as the truth. In real data it is often itself a product of past decisions, which this project does not model.
