# Decision memo: can offline NDCG decide which recommender to ship?

Generated by `python -m recsys_eval.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## The question
A personalised recommender is proposed to replace popularity. Offline, on logged clicks, can we tell whether it is better, and which one to ship?

## The rule, set before results were seen
Pass only if all three hold on the test window for the headline model, which is picked on the validation window as the personalised candidate (item_knn, svd, svd_ips) with the highest NDCG@10 against held-out clicks: (1) the 95% paired bootstrap interval over users on its NDCG@10 minus popularity's has a lower bound above zero; (2) the 95% paired interval on its true like rate at 10 minus popularity's has a lower bound above zero, where the true like rate is known only because this is a simulation; (3) across all seven models, the Spearman correlation between NDCG@10 on held-out clicks and the true like rate is at least 0.80, meaning the offline metric can be trusted to choose between models. The 0.80 bar is an assumption fixed before results.

## The call: FAIL
Headline model: item_knn.
- beats_popularity_on_observed_ndcg: True
- beats_popularity_on_true_like_rate: True
- offline_metric_orders_models_like_truth: False

Against popularity, item_knn gains +0.0104 NDCG@10 (+0.0066 to +0.0145) and +0.0278 true like rate (+0.0257 to +0.0295). Across the seven models, the offline metric and the true like rate have Spearman correlation -0.68. The bar was 0.8.

## Why passing the first two checks is not good news
Both checks compare against popularity, and popularity is worse than random on true like rate here (0.183 against 0.237). The headline, item_knn, has true like rate 0.211, below random. Beating a bad baseline is a low bar, and the gate was written with that baseline.

## The offline metric picked the wrong model
Validation NDCG@10 ranked the candidates item_knn > svd > svd_ips. By true like rate the best candidate is svd_ips (0.404, value captured +0.290), not item_knn (0.211, -0.045). The oracle, which knows what users like, ranks near the bottom on observed NDCG@10. The cause is exposure: held-out clicks come from a policy that shows popular items, so the metric rewards showing popular items. When the logging policy shows items evenly (exposure strength 0) the Spearman correlation is +0.93; at the default strength it is -0.68.

## What to do instead
Do not choose between models on NDCG against logged clicks from a popularity-biased policy. Run a small randomised exposure (or an online test) to get unbiased feedback, weight clicks by an estimated exposure propensity, and judge on that. In this project the inverse-popularity weighting (fixed exponent 0.5) took the true like rate from 0.347 (svd) to 0.404 (svd_ips) while observed NDCG@10 fell from 0.1039 to 0.0794. That is the trade a team has to accept knowingly. It is not a recommendation to use that exponent.

## What a wrong call costs
Shipping item_knn instead of svd_ips gives up 0.193 in like rate at 10, a drop of 48% of what svd_ips delivers. The value of a like is an assumption this project does not make.

## Where not to trust it
Everything. The world, the exposure policy and the users are simulated. The point is the mechanism, not the sizes. Items released after training cannot be recommended (6.5% of test clicks). Users with 1 to 4 prior clicks are 23 people, too few to read.

## Not done
No online experiment, no off-policy estimator, no neural or sequential model, no tuning, no feedback loop, no cold-start model, no real data.
