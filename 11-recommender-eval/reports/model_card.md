# Model card: recommender offline evaluation

Generated by `python -m recsys_eval.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for evaluating a recommender offline: a temporal split, ranking metrics, bootstrap intervals over users, and a check on whether the offline metric agrees with what users actually like. Not a production recommender.

## Data
Simulated: 1500 users, 600 items, 59081 logged clicks over 100 days. Test window: train on 50100 clicks before day 85, evaluate on 8981 clicks after it, for 1374 users with history and a held-out click. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
Items are shown by a policy that favours popular ones (strength 1.5). A user clicks a shown item with probability sigmoid(1.2 * affinity -1.5). Taste drifts by 0.5 over the horizon. All are assumptions about a made-up service.

## The traps, measured
- The observed metric rewards the logging policy. Models ordered by observed NDCG@10: recent_popularity > item_knn > popularity > svd > svd_ips > oracle > random. By true like rate: oracle > svd_ips > svd > random > item_knn > recent_popularity > popularity. Spearman correlation -0.68. The oracle, which knows what users like, scores 0.0351 against popularity's 0.1071. Held-out clicks were exposed by a policy that favours popular items, so a model that shows popular items is rewarded whether or not users like them.
- Popularity scores below random on true like rate. This is a selection effect, not a defect in the metric: popular items are liked about as much as any item on average, but once the items a user already clicked are removed, what remains is mostly popular items the user was shown and passed on.
- Cold start: 0.065 of test clicks are on items the training window never saw. No model trained on the past can recommend these. It caps recall.

## Limits
- Everything is simulated. Taste is a low-rank signal plus drift, exposure is a popularity softmax, and clicks are Bernoulli. The sizes of the effects describe this setup, not any real catalogue.
- True value is only known because the simulator knows it. In real life the equivalent is an online experiment, which this project does not run.
- Clicks are the only signal. There are no dwell times, ratings, purchases, sessions or context.
- Models are small classical baselines (popularity, item-kNN, SVD). No neural, sequential or two-tower model, and no tuning of ranks or neighbourhoods.
- The inverse-popularity weight uses a fixed exponent of 0.5 chosen before results. A real exposure model would estimate propensities.
- The 0.80 Spearman bar is an assumption. With seven models a correlation has a wide interval.
- Feedback loops (today's recommendations changing tomorrow's logs) are not simulated.
