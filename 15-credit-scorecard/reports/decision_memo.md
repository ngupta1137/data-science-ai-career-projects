## What this means
Recalibrating on the random sample beats the naive model and brings the predicted default rate within the band, but it does not beat the policy it would replace. In this simulation the old policy used a trait the underwriter saw and the data never recorded. A model on recorded features cannot recover that, so replacing judgement with the model gives that information away. The oracle earns +0.0605 per applicant, so better decisions exist; they need better data, not a better fit. By point estimate the best model here was `random_sample_only`.

## What would change the call
Recording what the underwriter used, or adding the model to the current policy rather than replacing it. Neither was tested here. A larger random sample improved calibration in the sample-size table, at a cost the model card prices.

## What a wrong call costs
Approving on an under-predicted default chance books loans that lose 0.6 each when they default. Declining good applicants gives up 0.15 each. Both are assumptions.
