# Model card: credit scorecard with reject bias

Generated by `python -m credit_score.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for building a default model when outcomes are seen only for loans you approved, and for deciding whether it should replace the current policy. Not a lending system and not a regulatory method.

## Data
40000 simulated past applications, 25076 approved (including 2057 approved at random). 40000 fresh applications where every outcome is known; the true default rate among all of them is 0.191 and the old policy approves 0.604. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
Margin 0.15 on a repaid loan, loss 0.6 on a default, so a loan is approved when the predicted default chance is below 0.200. Assumptions.

- `accepted_only`: gradient boosting on approved loans.
- `ipw`: the same, weighted by one over the chance of approval given recorded features.
- `random_sample_only`: logistic regression on the loans approved at random.
- `recalibrated`: accepted-only scores, recalibrated on the random sample. Headline, fixed before results.

## The traps, measured
- Evaluating on approved loans only: Gini 0.361 on old-approved applicants against 0.564 on all. Calibration 1.034 on old-approved and 0.692 on old-declined. On the loans you can check, the model looks calibrated. On the applicants it would newly approve, it is not.
- Weighting cannot fix what was not recorded: calibration on old-declined applicants is 0.692 for accepted-only, 0.701 with weighting and 0.883 after recalibration. Weighting by the chance of approval given recorded features fixes selection on those features only.
- A small unbiased sample: Gini 0.562 on the random sample alone against 0.564 recalibrated. The random sample alone is unbiased but too small to rank well; it is best used to recalibrate.
- The cost of learning: 840 of the 2057 random approvals were applicants the old policy declined. Their default rate was 0.352 and they earned -0.114 per loan, a cost of 0.0024 per past applicant.

## Limits
- Everything is simulated. The default model, the unrecorded trait, the old policy and the economics are chosen by the author, so the sizes describe this setup, not any lender.
- The test set knows whether every applicant would have defaulted. No lender has that. It is how this project can score the methods; in practice only the random sample gives a partial view.
- Loans are the same size and term. No pricing by risk, no recoveries over time, no prepayment, no economic cycle.
- The random sample approves applicants the old policy would have declined. That costs money and needs governance and, in many places, legal review. This project prices the cost and nothing else.
- Nothing here is a fair-lending or regulatory method. See project 08 for a group audit, and take legal questions to counsel.
- Models are untuned with settings fixed before results.
