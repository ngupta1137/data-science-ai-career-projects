# Decision memo: whom should we send the offer to?

Generated by `python -m uplift_eval.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## The question
Offer a discount to everyone, to nobody, or to a chosen group. Can a model trained on a randomised trial pick the group, and is it better than blanket offers?

## The rule, set before results were seen
Pass only if all three hold on the randomised test set for the headline model, which is picked on dev as the candidate (s_linear, s_gbm, t_gbm, ct_gbm) with the highest area under the uplift curve (AUUC): (1) the 95% bootstrap interval on the incremental profit per customer of treating when predicted uplift exceeds cost over value has a lower bound above zero, so it beats treating nobody; (2) the 95% paired interval on that profit minus the profit of treating everyone has a lower bound above zero, so it beats blanket offers; (3) the 95% interval on the Qini coefficient has a lower bound above zero, so the ranking beats random targeting. Profit is estimated by inverse-probability weighting with the known randomisation. The cost and value are assumptions fixed before results.

## The call: PASS
Headline learner: ct_gbm.
- beats_treating_nobody: True
- beats_treating_everybody: True
- ranking_beats_random_targeting: True

Estimated incremental profit per customer 3.97 (3.47 to 4.48). Against offering everyone: +1.70 (+1.36 to +2.06). Qini coefficient 0.0197 (0.0179 to 0.0215). On a large fresh population the true profit is 3.681, against 1.904 for offering everyone and 3.938 for a policy that knows each customer's effect.

## What the model gets right
35% of customers buy less when offered. Offering everyone loses 1.84 per customer on them. The headline offers to 4.5% of that group and to 90.3% of the group for whom the offer pays.

## Where this result is fragile
- Data. With 2000 training customers the best learner was s_linear (3.425) and the headline learner scored 2.705. With 20000 they are within 0.16 of each other. This pass is for a trial of 20000 customers and a strong effect.
- Choosing a learner on dev AUUC among four near-equal models is close to a coin flip. Their Qini intervals overlap.
- A response model that predicts who will buy earns 2.150 at a 30% budget against 3.166 for an uplift model.
- Training on past offers that followed buying signals cost between 0.43 and 0.78 in true profit per customer and gave a naive effect of 0.26 against a true 0.08. Use trial data.
- Absolute profit estimates from one trial all sat above their truth because they share the same outcomes. Compare policies by paired differences, as the gate does.

## What a wrong call costs
Offering to everyone costs the harmed group their purchases and the rest the offer cost. Offering to nobody gives up the profit the right group would bring. The money values are assumptions: 50 per conversion and 2 per offer. The cost table in the model card shows the share offered moving with the cost.

## Where not to trust it
Any real campaign. The effect, the hidden driver and the economics are all simulated. Top-decile uplift is under-predicted in this run (see the calibration table), so the highest-value customers are the least well estimated.

## Not done
No real data, no multiple offers or doses, no long-run effects, no cost of running the trial, no refit schedule. The decision is a one-off.
