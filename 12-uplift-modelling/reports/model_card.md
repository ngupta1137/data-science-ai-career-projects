# Model card: uplift modelling for an offer

Generated by `python -m uplift_eval.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for deciding whom to make an offer to from a randomised trial: uplift learners, the uplift (Qini) curve, profit by inverse-probability weighting, and a comparison with the true effect. Not a production targeting system.

## Data
Simulated customers with a known individual effect of the offer. 20000 train, 10000 dev and 20000 test customers from a randomised trial (offer given to 0.501 of the test set, conversion rate 0.218). A fresh population of 200000 customers gives the true value of each policy. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
A conversion is worth 50 and an offer costs 2, so an offer pays when the effect on the buying chance exceeds 0.040. Fixed-budget comparisons treat 30%. Both numbers are assumptions.

## The traps, measured
- A model of who will buy is not a model of who the offer changes. At a 30% budget the uplift model earns 3.166, the response model 2.150, random 0.571, the oracle 3.628. Mean true effect among those offered: 0.251 (uplift), 0.183 (response), 0.078 (everyone).
- Estimated profit is noisy and shares one sample's luck. For ct_gbm: estimated on training rows 3.911, on dev 4.113, best of the four on dev 4.279, on test 3.968; the true value is 3.681. Treat-all is estimated at 2.265 against a truth of 1.904, and the oracle at 4.189 against 3.938. Every absolute estimate sits above its truth, because they share the same outcomes. An estimate from the rows a model was fitted on, or the best of several candidates on the rows used to choose, is expected to be optimistic. Compare each with the true value. Absolute estimates from one sample share that sample's luck, so compare policies by paired differences.
- Offers that followed buying signals: the naive buyers-minus-non-buyers gap is 0.260 in the observational data and 0.075 in the trial; the true average effect is 0.078. When past offers went to likely buyers and some of what made them likely is not in the data, a learner reads that as the effect of the offer.

## Limits
- Everything is simulated. The effect of an offer, the size of the hidden driver and the economics are chosen by the author, so the sizes of the results describe this setup, not any retailer.
- The trial is large (20,000 customers per split) and the effect is strong and smooth. Real uplift is often small against noise and needs far more data than a response model.
- The value of a conversion and the cost of an offer are assumptions. The cost sensitivity table shows how the targeted share moves.
- Only a one-off binary offer with a binary outcome. No dose, timing, fatigue, multiple offers or long-run effects.
- The class-transformation learner is valid only because the trial gives the offer to exactly half. It would be wrong for other randomisation rates.
- The learners are untuned, with fixed settings chosen before results.
- True profit uses the simulator's effects. In practice the nearest equivalent is a later randomised holdout, which this project does not run.
