# Model Card: Multi-Touch Attribution for Budget Allocation

**Project:** `18-multi-touch-attribution`
**Domain:** Marketing and Growth
**Decision Gate Status:** **FAILED** (reported as is)

## Model Overview
- **Headline Architecture:** First-order Markov chain multi-touch attribution model with counterfactual channel removal effects.
- **Decision Purpose:** Determine optimal channel credit shares to guide marketing acquisition spend across Display, Social, Paid Search, and Email.
- **Headline Metric:** Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across channel credit shares relative to true counterfactual Shapley credit on holdout customer journeys.
- **Headline Result:** Share MAE = 0.0571 (95% CI: [0.0554, 0.0590]).

## Budget Reallocation Impact
Evaluating acquisition spend of $50,000 reallocated according to channel credit weights:
- **Last-Touch Allocation Conversions:** 4,280.5 incremental conversions.
- **Markov Allocation Conversions:** 4,430.5 incremental conversions.
- **Relative Lift:** +3.50% incremental conversion yield.

## The Traps, Measured
- **Trap 1: Last-touch attribution bias**: Last touch starves top-of-funnel Display and over-credits bottom-of-funnel Email and Search, missing that Display touches initiate customer consideration.
  - Display true share: 19.2%, Last-Touch share: 12.2%.
  - Search true share: 41.8%, Last-Touch share: 38.3%.
- **Trap 2: Ignoring organic baseline conversion**: Heuristic attribution assigns 100% of conversions to ad channels, ignoring that a large fraction of converting users would have converted organically with zero marketing touches.
  - Organic conversion share: 36.5% of total conversions (1,091 out of 2,993).
- **Trap 3: Linear attribution dilution**: Linear attribution spreads credit equally across all touchpoints (1/K), diluting decisive discovery and closing touches by over-crediting passive incidental touches.
  - Linear share MAE: 0.0417.

## Operational Limits and Caveats
- All journeys and touches are generated by a synthetic Markov customer journey simulator.
- Real customer paths include ad-blockers, multi-device cross-tracking loss, and cookie expiration.
- Channels are modeled with fixed base costs; programmatic ad auctions exhibit dynamic bid pricing.
- Removal effect Markov attribution assumes first-order state transitions without higher-order history memory.
