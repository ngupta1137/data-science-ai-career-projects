Generated by `python -m experiment_analysis.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for checking, by simulation, how often common A/B analysis methods are wrong. Not a substitute for an experimentation platform.

## Data
Simulated experiments with a known true effect (2000 per cell; 20000 A/A experiments at the headline cell). See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## The traps, measured
- Peeking: stopping at the first significant of 10 looks gives 0.194 (0.177 to 0.211) false positives instead of 0.05.
- Ten metrics, no correction: 0.398 (0.377 to 0.420) chance of at least one false positive. Holm: 0.051 (0.042 to 0.061).
- Skewed data, few users (200 per arm): raw false positive rate 0.048 (0.039 to 0.058) on lognormal versus 0.048 (0.039 to 0.058) on normal.
- Contaminated covariate: CUPED mean error -0.287 against a true effect of 0.1, interval coverage 0.000. With a clean covariate the error is -0.0011. The covariate moves with treatment, so CUPED removes part of the real effect. A covariate must be fixed before assignment.

## Where it fails
False positive rate outside nominal (95% interval excludes 0.05):
- cuped, normal, 200 per arm: false positive rate 0.068 (0.057 to 0.079)

Interval coverage clearly below 95%:
- raw, lognormal, 5000 per arm: coverage 0.940 (0.929 to 0.950)

12 method and cell pairs are checked for each list, so one or two flags can occur by chance. Treat a flag as a lead, not a proof, unless it is large or repeats across cells.

## Limits
- All data is simulated with a known truth. Real experiments have effects that vary by user, interference between users, and data problems this project does not model.
- The outcome is one number per user with a clean pre-period metric. Real metrics are noisier and pre-period data is often missing.
- Tests use a normal approximation. Intervals are about the simulation, so they show how reliable a method is under these assumptions only.
- Only equally spaced looks and Bonferroni are measured. Alpha-spending and always-valid methods are designed, not built.
- No sample ratio mismatch checks, no novelty effects, no network effects, no variance from repeated measures.
