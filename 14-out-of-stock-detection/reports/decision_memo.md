## What this means
The value-ranked list earns more than the current rule, which loses money at this budget. It fails the precision check: most visits find a full shelf. By point estimate the best non-oracle policy here was `expected_value_naive_rate`, though the intervals of the three value-aware policies overlap, and the oracle shows +9.84 is available.

## Why it fails
The model is overconfident. Visited shelves had a predicted chance of being empty of 0.733, and 0.136 were. Two measured facts point the same way: the corrected sales rate runs high (median 1.175 of the truth), and the prior of 0.05 is above the 0.033 share of shelf-days that were empty once visits began. How much each contributes was not measured, and ranking thousands of flagged shelves by a noisy score may add a winner's curse on top. The budget table agrees: at 50 visits a day the same policy earns +5.83, so the marginal visits at the full budget lose money even though the model says they pay.

## What to do next
Calibrate the chance of an empty shelf on audited visits before trusting the visit-cost cut-off, lower the prior as visits clear shelves, and cap visits until the calibration holds. Then rerun this gate unchanged.

## What a wrong call costs
A wasted visit costs 3. A missed empty shelf costs its daily margin until someone notices. Both are assumptions here.
