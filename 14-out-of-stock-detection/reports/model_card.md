# Model card: out-of-stock detection

Generated by `python -m oos_detect.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for turning point-of-sale data into a short daily list of shelves to check, under a fixed visit budget. Not a store operations system.

## Data
50 simulated stores, 10000 shelves, 60 history days without visits and 60 evaluation days. With no visits, 2314 empty-shelf episodes start in the evaluation days. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
Margin 1 per unit, visit cost 3, 150 visits a day across the chain, prior share of empty shelves 0.05. All are assumptions. Every policy faces the same demand and the same empty-shelf episodes.

## Policies
Net value is margin saved over no visits minus visit cost, per store per day, with a store-bootstrap 95% interval.

| expected_value_naive_rate | +1.38 (+0.24 to +2.53) | 3.45 | 150.0 | 724 | 0.144 | 0.459 | 1 |

- `zero_run_rule`: flag after 3 days with no sales, longest gap first.
- `poisson_alarm`: flag when the gap is very unlikely for the shelf's rate.
- `expected_value`: chance the shelf is empty times the margin it would lose, visited if that beats the visit cost. Headline, fixed before results.
- `expected_value_naive_rate`: the same with the rate estimated from all history days.
- `oracle`: knows which shelves are empty. Upper bound.

## The traps, measured
- Zero sales read as zero demand: median naive rate is 0.972 of the true rate and the corrected rate is 1.175. Net value +1.38 with the naive rate and +1.11 with the corrected one. In this simulation the bias is small and the correction overshoots.
- One rule for every shelf: the zero-day rule flags 2515 shelves a day against 150 visits, with precision 0.180. Three days without a sale is normal for a slow seller and a long gap for a fast one, so one rule floods reps with slow-seller alerts.
- Alarm without value: the alarm has precision 0.211 and net value +1.22. A shelf can be almost surely empty and still not worth a visit if it sells little.

## Limits
- Everything is simulated. Sales rates, how often shelves go empty, how long they stay empty, the margin and the visit cost are chosen by the author.
- Demand has no weekday pattern, season or promotions, which in real data create zero-sales runs that are not empty shelves.
- The prior share of empty shelves is set close to the simulator's true value. In practice it comes from shelf audits and is less accurate.
- A visit always finds and fixes an empty shelf. Real reps miss some, and some empty shelves cannot be refilled because there is no stock in the back room.
- Shelves are independent. Real empty shelves cluster by store, delivery day and supplier.
- Intervals resample stores. They do not cover a different simulator.
