# Model card: house price regression

Generated by `python -m house_price.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for how to evaluate a regression model honestly. Not for valuing real property or making decisions about people.

## Data
California Housing, 1990 census block groups. 20,640 rows. 992 rows (4.8%) are censored at the target cap. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Split
spatial group split, 0.5 degree cells. 14,896 train rows in 140 cells, 5,744 test rows in 36 cells. No cell appears on both sides.

## Results
Units are $100,000 (1990 dollars). Cross-validation is grouped by cell, on train rows only. Test rows were scored once after the headline model was chosen by CV.

| Model | CV RMSE | Test RMSE | Test MAE | Test R2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline_mean | 1.1586 | 1.1657 | 0.8719 | -0.0266 |
| baseline_median | 1.1818 | 1.2368 | 0.8750 | -0.1557 |
| ridge | 0.8334 | 0.8154 | 0.5571 | 0.4978 |

Headline: **hist_gradient_boosting**, test RMSE 0.6799 ($67,987), 95% interval 0.4368 to 0.7894 (cluster bootstrap over spatial cells, 1000 resamples). Best baseline: baseline_mean, test RMSE 1.1657.

## The leakage trap
Same model and hyperparameters. Random split test RMSE 0.4880. Spatial split test RMSE 0.6799. Difference +0.1919. The two test sets contain different rows, so this is a comparison of evaluation designs, not of identical rows.

## Where it fails
Worst segment with at least 30 test rows: **price_band:censored_at_cap**. Overall bias (predicted minus actual): -0.2500. Full tables are in `results/error_analysis.json`.

## Limits
- Targets at or above the cap are censored in the source data. The model cannot learn true values above the cap.
- The interval resamples spatial cells. It does not capture uncertainty from the choice of split.
- Metrics are in units of $100,000 in 1990 dollars. They say nothing about current prices.
- Spatial cells are 0.5 degrees. Cells near the same city can still share information across the cell border.
