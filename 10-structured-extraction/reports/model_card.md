# Model card: structured extraction from invoices

Generated by `python -m extraction_eval.report` from `results/metrics.json`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. A reference for evaluating document field extraction: canonical scoring, document-level accuracy, failure taxonomy, unseen templates and OCR noise. Not a production extractor.

## Data
Generated invoices with five fields (vendor, invoice number, date, total, currency). 2000 train, 600 dev and 1200 test documents from seen templates A, B, C; 600 test documents from each unseen template E, G. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Extractors
- `first_match`: the first candidate of each type. A baseline.
- `template_regex`: hand-written rules for templates A, B and C, covering label variants that appear in at least 30% of their documents. It returns nothing when no rule matches.
- `span_ranker`: a logistic regression that scores every candidate span from the words around it and its position, trained on the seen templates. It returns nothing when its best score is below 0.5.

## The traps, measured
- Field scores hide document errors: for the headline, micro F1 is 0.867 on unseen templates while only 0.313 of documents are fully right. On seen templates the two are 1.000 and 1.000.
- Random split versus a held-out template: a random split of documents from seen templates gives 1.000. Training on two templates and testing on the third gives A 0.848, B 0.812, C 0.000 (mean 0.553). Training on two templates and testing on the third is the honest estimate for a new customer's format. A random split of documents from the same templates is not.
- Silent errors: wrong values that still parse as a valid date, amount or currency pass any schema check.

## Limits
- All documents are generated. They are far cleaner and more regular than real invoices, so the near-perfect results on seen templates say the task is easy to learn in this setting, not that extraction is solved.
- The unseen templates were written by the same author as the seen ones, and the rules were written knowing the seen templates only. The author was not blind to the unseen ones. A real shift is usually less tidy.
- No LLM was used. An LLM extractor with a schema is designed, not built; the scoring code here applies to its output unchanged.
- OCR noise is a crude character swap, not a real recogniser. Layout errors, skewed scans and tables spanning pages are not modelled.
- Dates are assumed day first for slashes. A month-first source would be silently misread.
- One currency per document, two-decimal amounts, five fields, no line items extracted.
- The 0.90 and 0.70 bars are assumptions. Change them and the verdict can change.
