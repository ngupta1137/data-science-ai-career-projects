# Decision memo: does this pipeline clear the minimum bar?

Generated by `python -m rag_eval.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## The question
Does retrieval plus a simple reader beat a system that always refuses? This is a minimum bar, not a ship decision.

## The rule, set before results were seen
Pass only if both hold on the test questions: (1) the lower bound of the 95% bootstrap interval on end-to-end correctness is above the correctness of the always-abstain policy (which equals the share of unanswerable questions); (2) unless the headline retriever is BM25 itself, the lower bound of the 95% paired bootstrap interval on the MRR gain over BM25 is above zero, so that any extra complexity is shown to pay for itself.

## The call: PASS
Headline retriever lsa, test MRR 0.827 (95% interval 0.711 to 0.924). End-to-end correct 0.481 (95% interval 0.352 to 0.630) against 0.296 for always abstaining. Condition 1, end to end beats always abstain: True. Condition 2, retrieval gain over BM25: True.

## Refusal
Of 16 unanswerable test questions, the system correctly refused 1. Passing the gate means it beats always refusing on the combined score. It does not mean it refuses well. The gate is a low bar and a reader should treat it that way.

## What a wrong answer costs
An answer given to an unanswerable question looks the same as a correct one to the reader of the answer. In this run the most frequent failure is **answered_unanswerable** (15 questions). The reader alone, given the right documents, scores 0.947, so the losses are upstream of reading.

## Where not to trust it
Refusal is the weakest part. The confidence signal is word overlap between the question and one sentence. A question that names a real policy but asks about something it does not state overlaps as much as a real question. See the taxonomy and `results/error_analysis.json`.
