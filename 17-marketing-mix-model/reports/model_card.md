# Model card: marketing mix modeling and budget reallocation

Generated by `python -m marketing_mix.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## Intended use
Learning. An econometric reference for evaluating marketing mix modeling (MMM) with carryover adstock and Hill saturation against naive linear regression. Not a production media buying system or commercial mix optimizer.

## Data
104 training weeks and 52 holdout evaluation weeks. Total historical budget averages $82,707.9 per week across TV, Search, and Social. See `data/DATA_CARD.md`.

## Assumptions
Baseline sales $100,000 per week. Channels: tv, search, social. Minimum acceptable holdout R^2 0.75, minimum marginal ROI rank correlation 0.80, minimum budget reallocation gain 5%. All candidate models are evaluated on identical holdout weeks.

- `naive_linear`: Linear regression on contemporaneous weekly spend (no adstock, no saturation).
- `adstock_only`: Geometric adstock decay fit per channel with linear spend response.
- `saturation_only`: Hill saturation diminishing returns fit per channel without carryover decay.
- `adstock_hill_mmm`: Full marketing mix model with joint geometric adstock and Hill saturation. Headline model, fixed before results.
- `oracle`: Known truth simulator response curves and parameters. Upper bound benchmark.

## The traps, measured
- Ignoring adstock decay: TV true carryover decay alpha is 0.60 (half-life 1.36 weeks). Naive linear regression assumes zero carryover (alpha 0.0, half-life 0.0 weeks). Headline MMM estimated alpha is 0.222. Naive linear regression assumes zero carryover decay (half-life 0.0 weeks), missing TV's multi-week brand persistence (true half-life 1.36 weeks).
- Ignoring saturation: At 2x historical average Search spend ($38,033.0), linear assumptions project a 2.00x lift increase. True Hill diminishing returns yield only 1.321x lift. Under linear assumptions, doubling Search spend projects a 2.00x revenue increase, whereas true Hill diminishing returns yield diminishing marginal return.
- Correlated spend bias: TV and Search spend correlation is 0.563. In naive OLS, multicollinearity inverts Search's slope to -0.0606, cutting Search allocation to $5,000.0 compared to balanced MMM allocation of $20,595.8. TV and Search spend are synchronized; naive OLS suffers multicollinearity, inverting Search's slope and cutting Search spend to minimum compared to balanced MMM allocation.

## Limits
- All data is generated by an econometric simulator with known adstock and Hill saturation.
- Real marketing channels exhibit competitive bidding dynamics and creative fatigue not modeled here.
- Observation noise is additive Gaussian without macroeconomic supply shocks or stock-outs.
- Bootstrap intervals resample holdout weeks; they do not cover structural shifts in channel effectiveness.
