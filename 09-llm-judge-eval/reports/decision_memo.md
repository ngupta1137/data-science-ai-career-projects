# Decision memo: can this judge replace human labels for ranking systems?

Generated by `python -m llm_judge_eval.report` from `results/`. Do not edit by hand.

## The question
Can a pairwise LLM judge be used in place of human preference labels to rank candidate systems, and if not, how should it be used?

## The rule, set before results were seen
Pass only if all three hold on the test set for the headline judge, which is picked on dev as the full-coverage judge candidate (single_order or swap_calibrated) with the higher agreement with the human consensus: (1) the lower 95% bound on agreement with the human consensus is at least 0.80, the level Zheng et al. 2023 report for strong judges; (2) the 95% interval on agreement minus the agreement of always picking the longer answer has a lower bound above zero; (3) in the system ranking, the verbose system V, whose true quality is below S4 and S5, beats S4 or S5 head to head in at most 5% of prompt-level bootstrap resamples. The 0.80 bar and the 5% limit are assumptions fixed before results.

## The call: FAIL
Headline judge: single_order.
- agreement_lower_bound_at_least_floor: False
- beats_longer_answer_baseline: True
- verbose_system_not_over_ranked: False

Agreement with the human consensus on test: 0.761, 95% interval 0.746 to 0.776. Against always picking the longer answer: +0.174 (+0.153 to +0.194). In the system ranking, V beats a truly better system in 1.000 of resamples.

## How the headline was chosen, and what that missed
Dev agreement was 0.7807 for single_order and 0.7807 for swap_calibrated. The rule picks the higher and breaks ties by list order. On test, swap_calibrated scored 0.782 against 0.761. Choosing between two judges whose dev scores differ by less than the noise is a coin flip, and the rule was not changed after seeing this.

## What would actually work
Call both orders and keep only the verdicts that agree. That keeps 0.727 of pairs at 0.873 agreement with the consensus (interval 0.858 to 0.886). The rest go to humans. Breaking splits with a coin does not help: it has the same expected agreement as one call. The cost of triage is the human labelling of 0.273 of pairs.

## What a wrong call costs
The risk is shipping the wrong system. Under the single-call judge, the verbose system V ranks 1 of 6 while its true rank is 3. Under always-pick-longer it ranks 1. Neither cost can be priced here, because the cost of shipping a worse system is an assumption.

## Where not to trust it
Pairs where the two answers are close in quality: the headline judge agrees with truth 0.555 of the time there, and the consensus itself only 0.584. No agreement number can be certified on those pairs. Also anything outside this simulation: the judge, the humans and the biases are all planted.
