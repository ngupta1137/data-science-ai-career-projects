## The Question
Should our algorithmic pricing engine estimate price elasticity using standard historical regression (Naive OLS) or upgrade to Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) using wholesale cost shocks as an instrumental variable?

## The Rule (Pre-registered Before Results)
Pass only if all three hold for the headline Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) instrumental variable model, fixed before results: (1) the absolute elasticity estimation error against true counterfactual elasticity on holdout products is below 0.25 units (with 95% bootstrap CI upper bound below 0.35); (2) the 2SLS model achieves strictly lower estimation error than naive OLS; (3) gross profit generated by pricing under the 2SLS elasticity model recovers at least 90% of theoretical oracle maximum profit on holdout products, whereas naive OLS pricing recovers strictly less than 80% of oracle profit.

The candidate Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) pricing engine **PASSED** the pre-registered decision gate.

## What This Means
1. **Never fit price elasticity using raw retail transactions:** Retail prices rise during peak demand surges (price-shock correlation r = 0.48). A naive regression conflates demand shifts with price movements, concluding that demand is inelastic (|beta| = 0.47 < 1.0).
2. **Naive pricing destroys gross profit:** Because naive regression reports inelastic demand, automated optimizers attempt to drive prices toward infinity or arbitrary ceiling caps (4.5x unit cost). When prices quadruple on truly elastic products, customer volume collapses, destroying $111,157.24 (27.7%) in gross margin.
3. **Wholesale cost shocks provide clean identification:** Commodity input costs and freight tariffs shift retail prices without directly altering consumer utility, yielding a strong first stage (F = 2601.7 >> 50.0) that recovers true causal price sensitivity.

## Where This Is Fragile
- **Instrument Validity (Exclusion Restriction):** The wholesale cost instrument assumes supplier cost increases do not directly alter consumer brand perception or willingness to pay, except through retail shelf prices.
- **Constant Elasticity Assumption:** The model assumes constant price elasticity across price bands. Extreme price shifts outside historical training support may exhibit non-linear demand kinks.
- **Unmodeled Competitor Retaliation:** The simulation optimizes prices given static competitor price distributions without modeling multi-agent dynamic Bertrand price wars.

## What a Wrong Call Costs
- **Retaining Naive OLS:** Causes continuous revenue destruction across elastic catalog categories by imposing exorbitant markups that suppress unit velocity.
- **Rejecting 2SLS:** Sacrifices over 25 percentage points in recoverable gross profit margin compared to disciplined causal pricing.
